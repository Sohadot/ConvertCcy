Morocco:
Foreign Currency Rules

Currency: MAD — Moroccan Dirham ISO: MA / MAR Last reviewed: 2026-05-02 Lifecycle: needs_hardening Indexing: blocked

Country Overview

Morocco remains under a regulated foreign-exchange framework administered by the Office des Changes. The Office des Changes announced that IGOC 2026 enters into force on 1 January 2026 and described it as a clearer framework that continues a gradual and well-controlled liberalization while preserving the Kingdom's external balances.

Rules at a Glance

Bringing foreign currency into the country
Bills of exchange, means of payment, and financial instruments are subject to customs declaration at entry when their value is equal to or greater than MAD 100,000. For amounts below MAD 100,000, the customs declaration is optional.
Taking foreign currency out of the country
The reviewed official materials state that the import declaration is required to justify the origin of foreign currency in re-export operations. Users should therefore retain the import declaration and any bank or exchange documentation when planning to re-export foreign currency.
Cash declaration threshold
MAD 100,000 or more for effects of commerce, means of payment, and financial instruments brought into the territory subject to the foreign-exchange rules reviewed here.
Rules for residents holding foreign currency
The reviewed Office des Changes materials and IGOC 2026 do not establish a general public rule giving ordinary Moroccan residents unrestricted foreign-currency-account access. They expressly preserve and expand targeted foreign-currency account use for exporters and specific authorized cases. Residents should verify account eligibility directly under IGOC 2026.
Rules for non-residents
Office des Changes states that accredited intermediaries may open currency accounts and accounts in convertible dirhams under the conditions set by the relevant clauses, and that such accounts may be opened in the name of foreign resident or non-resident private individuals and foreign legal persons or their representations in Morocco.
Business invoicing and settlement in foreign currency
IGOC 2026 updates the framework for investment, exports, e-commerce, business travel, service imports, and hedging instruments. Businesses should verify transaction-specific invoicing, settlement, and authorization requirements directly in the applicable IGOC 2026 sections and with the authorized intermediary bank before execution.
Exchange controls and approval requirements
Morocco remains under exchange regulations administered by the Office des Changes. According to the official IGOC 2026 release, the 2026 framework continues a gradual and well-controlled liberalization while preserving the Kingdom's external balances.
Banking and conversion practicality
The reviewed Office des Changes materials explicitly connect certain foreign-currency operations to customs declarations, change receipts, and authorized intermediary banks.

Practical Summaries

Traveler summary

Foreign means of payment and financial instruments reaching MAD 100,000 or more at entry are subject to customs declaration. The import and export of dirhams are prohibited in principle, but travellers may carry banknotes not exceeding MAD 2,000. The import declaration is required to justify certain later operations, including some account funding and re-export cases.

Business summary

IGOC 2026 introduces targeted measures relating to investment, exports, e-commerce, business travel, service imports, hedging instruments, and individuals.
