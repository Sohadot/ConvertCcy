Turkey — Currency Passage Brief

TRY · Turkish Lira · Eastern Europe

Declaration threshold
TRY 185,000 (Turkish lira cash)
EUR 10,000 (foreign banknotes / efektif, or the equivalent in another foreign currency)
Exchange controls
Türkiye operates a floating exchange-rate regime with no nominal or real exchange-rate target; the reviewed official sources do not establish a single general foreign-exchange authorisation regime, while specific regulated layers remain distinct — resident/non-resident FX account and transfer rules under Decree No. 32, outbound physical-cash declaration triggers, banking-channel-only source/purpose categories, domestic FX-contract restrictions, export-proceeds repatriation/sale requirements, and transaction-specific reporting. Absence of a located restriction is not evidence of absence. The floating regime is a monetary fact, not itself an exchange-control rule.

For travellers

Carrying cash in or out

Bringing foreign currency into Türkiye is generally free under Decree No. 32: there is no general mandatory numeric declaration threshold for ordinary passenger-carried inbound cash; a traveller may declare voluntarily, and Turkish Customs may request an explanation of the amount, source and purpose under the anti-money-laundering framework (Law No. 5549). Taking cash out is different and declaration-triggered: carrying Turkish lira exceeding TRY 185,000, or foreign banknotes (efektif) exceeding EUR 10,000 or the equivalent, out of Türkiye requires a declaration on the Customs Cash Declaration Form (Nakit Beyan Formu). Separately, certain cash categories may not be carried by passengers by reason of source or purpose and must instead move through the banking system. These declaration triggers are not a maximum amount that may be exported.

  • Bringing currency in: Bringing foreign currency into Türkiye is generally free under Decree No. 32 Art. 4(a) (yurda döviz ithali serbesttir); the reviewed sources establish no general mandatory numeric declaration threshold for ordinary passenger-carried inbound cash. A traveller may make a voluntary declaration, and Turkish Customs may require an explanation and documentation of the amount, its source and its purpose under the anti-money-laundering framework (Law No. 5549) — an onboarding/compliance explanation layer, not the legal source of the import freedom. Separately, certain cash categories are, by reason of source or purpose, not permitted to be carried into Türkiye by passengers and must instead move through the banking system (Ministry of Trade Cash Controls Circular). The reviewed sources do not establish a symmetric inbound EUR 10,000 declaration ceiling.
  • Taking currency out: Taking cash out of Türkiye is declaration-triggered. Carrying Turkish lira exceeding (aşan) TRY 185,000 out of the country requires a declaration on the Customs Cash Declaration Form (Nakit Beyan Formu) (Decree No. 32 Art. 3(d); Communiqué No. 2008-32/34 Art. 3(5)). Carrying foreign banknotes (efektif) exceeding EUR 10,000 or the equivalent in other foreign currency out of the country likewise requires a Nakit Beyan Formu declaration (Decree No. 32 Art. 4(f); Communiqué No. 2008-32/34 Art. 4(2)). The binding word is 'aşan' (exceeding, strictly '>'). These figures are declaration triggers, not a maximum exportable amount; separately, certain cash categories may not be carried out by passengers by reason of source or purpose and must move through the banking system. The pre-March-2025 TRY 25,000 figure is superseded and is not used.
  • Declaration: On exit the cross-border cash-declaration triggers are: Turkish lira exceeding TRY 185,000, and foreign banknotes (efektif) exceeding EUR 10,000 or the equivalent — in each case declared to Turkish Customs on the Cash Declaration Form (Nakit Beyan Formu). The binding operator is 'exceeding' (aşan, strictly '>'). On entry there is no general mandatory numeric declaration threshold: foreign-currency import is generally free under Decree No. 32 Art. 4(a), declaration is voluntary, and Customs may require an explanation of amount, source and purpose under Law No. 5549 as a compliance layer. Legal basis: Decree No. 32 (Art. 3(d), Art. 4(f)) and Communiqué No. 2008-32/34 (Art. 3(5), Art. 4(2)); enforcement by the Ministry of Trade / Turkish Customs (Customs Law No. 4458). This customs declaration is distinct from the banking-channel-only cash categories, from the anti-money-laundering explanation layer, and from any notion of an export ceiling.
Residency

Resident vs non-resident

  • Residents: Under Decree No. 32 Art. 4(b), persons resident in Türkiye (Türkiye'de yerleşik kişiler — Decree Art. 2) may generally possess foreign exchange, buy and sell foreign exchange through authorised channels, hold foreign exchange in bank foreign-currency (döviz tevdiat) accounts, hold and use foreign banknotes (efektif), and dispose of foreign exchange through banks domestically and abroad. Separately, Decree No. 32 Art. 19(1) provides that the Central Bank and banks may open foreign-currency deposit accounts (döviz tevdiat hesapları) and precious-metal depot accounts in the name of persons resident in Türkiye; account holders may freely dispose of those accounts; interest is freely agreed between bank and account holder; and principal and interest transfers are met by banks from their own resources. Art. 4(b) is the resident FX possession/use/holding layer; Art. 19(1) is the FX deposit-account opening and disposition framework. Neither provision is a Central Bank approval regime for ordinary holding, and neither establishes an unconditional right to every banking product or service. Customer-due-diligence / KYC obligations are a separate operational/compliance onboarding layer and are not the legal source of this account framework. Transaction-specific rules (for example on foreign-currency-denominated domestic contracts or reporting) may separately apply.
  • Non-residents: Under Decree No. 32 Art. 4(d), persons resident abroad (Dışarıda yerleşik kişiler — Decree Art. 2) may buy and sell foreign exchange with banks, authorised institutions (yetkili müesseseler), the PTT, precious-metal intermediary institutions and brokerages. Under Art. 4(e), persons resident in Türkiye and persons resident abroad may freely cause foreign-exchange transfers abroad through banks. Separately, Decree No. 32 Art. 19(1) provides that the Central Bank and banks may open foreign-currency deposit accounts (döviz tevdiat hesapları) and precious-metal depot accounts in the name of persons resident abroad; account holders may freely dispose of those accounts; interest is freely agreed between bank and account holder; and principal and interest transfers are met by banks from their own resources. This is the FX deposit-account opening framework for non-residents — not a claim that non-residents 'transfer more freely', and not an unconditional right to every Turkish-lira or foreign-currency banking product. Customer-due-diligence / KYC obligations remain a separate operational/compliance onboarding layer. Transaction-specific restrictions and reporting may separately apply.
For business

Invoicing, settlement & conversion

Cross-border business payments run through banks and authorised institutions under Decree No. 32; the reviewed sources do not establish a general Central Bank approval requirement. Several distinct rules apply and are not one 'approval' regime: foreign-currency-denominated domestic contracts between residents are restricted with enumerated exceptions (Decree No. 32 Art. 4(g)); exporters are subject to an export-proceeds repatriation regime under the current CBRT Export Circular, and under that circular at least 35% of export proceeds documented through İBKB/DAB are subject to sale through the intermediary bank to the CBRT through 31 January 2027 inclusive — the percentage is time-sensitive and must be reverified after that date; and import payments, dividends, foreign-direct-investment proceeds and other transfers follow their own transaction-specific rules and reporting. The reviewed sources do not establish an unrestricted right to invoice, settle or repatriate in any currency without condition.

  • Invoicing & settlement: Cross-border business payments and settlement run through banks and authorised institutions under Decree No. 32; the reviewed sources do not establish a general Central Bank approval requirement, and the earlier on-file 'capital repatriation and certain payments require CBRT approval' claim is not asserted. Several distinct rules apply and must not be conflated: (i) foreign-currency-denominated domestic contracts between persons resident in Türkiye are restricted (Decree No. 32 Art. 4(g) and its implementing communiqué), with enumerated exceptions, so many domestic contract prices must be set in Turkish lira; (ii) exporters are subject to an export-proceeds repatriation regime under the current CBRT Export Circular (İhracat Genelgesi), under which export proceeds must be brought into Türkiye through banks and, separately, at least 35% of export proceeds documented through İBKB/DAB are subject to sale through the intermediary bank to the CBRT through 31 January 2027 inclusive (GEÇİCİ MADDE 4; base EK MADDE 3 is 25%; Ministry of Treasury and Finance instruction dated 30 July 2026) — the percentage is time-sensitive and must be reverified after that date; and (iii) import payments, dividends, foreign-direct-investment proceeds and other transfers follow their own transaction-specific rules and any reporting requirements, which are not general approval requirements. The reviewed sources do not establish an unrestricted right to invoice, settle or repatriate in any currency without condition.
  • Exchange controls: Türkiye does not operate a single general exchange-control authorisation regime in the reviewed sources; instead several distinct, layered elements apply and must not be conflated: a floating exchange-rate regime with no target (CBRT), kept separate from controls; generally liberal foreign-exchange holding by residents (Art. 4(b)) and FX deposit-account opening for residents and non-residents (Art. 19(1)); transfers abroad through banks (Art. 4(e)); outbound physical-cash declaration triggers (Turkish lira exceeding TRY 185,000; efektif exceeding EUR 10,000 — Nakit Beyan Formu); source/purpose cash categories that must move through the banking system; a restriction on foreign-currency-denominated domestic contracts (Art. 4(g)); an export-proceeds repatriation/surrender regime under the CBRT Export Circular; and transaction-specific capital-movement and reporting rules. Bank-level macroprudential foreign-exchange measures (reserve requirements, conversion incentives) are prudential rules applied to banks and are not customer currency-passage restrictions. The reviewed sources do not establish that Türkiye is 'fully liberalised' or has 'no controls', and absence of a located restriction is not evidence that none exists.
  • Banking & conversion: Foreign-exchange conversion is available through banks and through authorised institutions (yetkili müesseseler) — joint-stock exchange companies licensed by the Ministry of Treasury and Finance under Decree No. 32 and classified by permitted activity — and, where applicable, other specifically authorised channels such as the PTT. Licensing of authorised exchange institutions sits with the Ministry of Treasury and Finance, not the Central Bank. Documentation and underlying-transaction requirements may apply to larger transactions, and the availability of a particular currency, service or rate is not guaranteed by any institution.
