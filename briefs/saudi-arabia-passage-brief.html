Saudi Arabia — Currency Passage Brief

SAR · Saudi Riyal · Middle East

Declaration threshold
SAR 40,000 (currency (Saudi or foreign), bearer negotiable instruments, gold bullion, precious metals, gemstones and worked jewellery — combined total value reaching or exceeding this amount, or the equivalent in another currency)

For travellers

Carrying cash in or out

Travellers entering or leaving Saudi Arabia must submit a declaration to the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority (ZATCA) when the currency, bearer negotiable instruments, gold bullion, precious metals, gemstones or worked jewellery they carry reach or exceed SAR 40,000 (or the equivalent in another currency) in total. The obligation is symmetric on entry and exit and is filed through the prescribed ZATCA declaration mechanism.

  • Bringing currency in: A traveller entering Saudi Arabia must declare to ZATCA any currency (Saudi or foreign), bearer negotiable instruments, gold bullion, precious metals, gemstones or worked jewellery whose total value reaches or exceeds SAR 40,000 or the equivalent in another currency, using the prescribed declaration mechanism. Below that threshold no declaration is required. The declaration duty derives from Article 23/1 of the Implementing Regulation to the Anti-Money Laundering Law.
  • Taking currency out: A traveller leaving Saudi Arabia must likewise declare currency, bearer negotiable instruments, gold bullion, precious metals, gemstones or worked jewellery whose total value reaches or exceeds SAR 40,000 or the equivalent (Article 23/1 of the Implementing Regulation to the Anti-Money Laundering Law); the obligation is symmetric with entry. The reviewed official sources do not establish a separate Saudi Central Bank permission or export cap on physically carrying currency out beyond this declaration duty.
  • Declaration: The declaration threshold is a total value reaching or exceeding SAR 40,000 (or the equivalent in another currency) in currency, bearer negotiable instruments, gold bullion, precious metals, gemstones or worked jewellery, for a traveller entering or leaving the Kingdom (Article 23/1 of the Implementing Regulation to the Anti-Money Laundering Law), declared to ZATCA through the prescribed mechanism. This anti-money-laundering border declaration is distinct from bank AML/KYC reporting and from SAMA's account-eligibility rules.
Residency

Resident vs non-resident

  • Residents: Under SAMA's Account Opening Rules, bank customers may open accounts in any available foreign currency and deposit and withdraw in that currency; where the currency is unavailable, payment may be made in Saudi riyal. Eligibility to open and operate an account depends on the account-opening conditions for the customer's category and on AML/KYC requirements; the reviewed sources do not establish an unconditional right for any person to hold a particular account.
  • Non-residents: Under SAMA's Account Opening Rules, banks may not open a Saudi-riyal account, a foreign-currency account, or any other account for non-Saudi, non-GCC natural persons who do not reside in Saudi Arabia unless written approval from the Ministry of Interior or the Ministry of Foreign Affairs is obtained through SAMA. Saudi citizens residing abroad and GCC nationals are addressed by their own account-opening rules within the same framework. The reviewed sources therefore do not support a general statement that non-residents may open or operate accounts freely; eligibility is category-specific and, for non-Saudi/non-GCC non-residents, conditional on the prescribed approval.
For business

Invoicing, settlement & conversion

Foreign-currency accounts and conversion are available through licensed banks and SAMA-licensed money-exchange businesses, subject to SAMA's Account Opening Rules and to AML/KYC requirements. The reviewed official sources establish account-eligibility conditions by customer category rather than an unrestricted right to hold or transfer currency; ordinary commercial settlement through licensed institutions is supported, while unrestricted capital repatriation is not established by the reviewed sources.

  • Invoicing & settlement: Cross-border business payments and currency conversion run through licensed banks and SAMA-licensed money-exchange businesses, subject to AML/KYC and SAMA's account-opening rules. The reviewed official sources support ordinary commercial settlement through these licensed institutions but do not establish an unrestricted right to invoice, settle or repatriate in any currency without condition; invoice-currency and repatriation arrangements should be verified for the specific transaction.
  • Exchange controls: The Saudi Riyal is maintained at a fixed exchange rate against the US Dollar (SAR 3.75) by the Saudi Central Bank (SAMA); this is a monetary-regime fact and does not by itself establish the presence or absence of exchange or capital-movement controls. The reviewed official sources establish a licensed foreign-exchange market (banks and SAMA-licensed money-exchange businesses), account-eligibility conditions by customer category, a cross-border AML declaration regime and AML/KYC requirements; they do not establish a general exchange-control authorisation regime for buying, holding or transferring currency. Absence of a located restriction is not evidence that none exists, and narrower sectoral or transaction-specific rules may apply.
  • Banking & conversion: Currency conversion and transfers are available through licensed banks and SAMA-licensed money-exchange businesses, within their respective licensed scope. Any client conditions or limits that SAMA applies to money-exchange businesses are specific to those licensees and are not general limits on bank customers. Availability of a particular currency, service or price is not guaranteed by any institution.
Official sources
  • Saudi Central Bank (SAMA) — 'SAMA Affirms Commitment to Exchange Rate Policy' (5 Apr 2020): directly states pegging the Saudi riyal to the US dollar and maintaining the official rate of 3.75 riyals to the dollar as an anchor of monetary and financial stability · primary
  • SAMA Rulebook — Account Opening Rules (foreign-currency accounts; customer-category eligibility; non-Saudi/non-GCC non-resident written-approval requirement) · primary
  • SAMA Rulebook — Rules Regulating Money Changing Business (SAMA Decision No. 4686, 1441H/2020; in force): licensing of money-changing business (Art. 6); permitted activities (Art. 11) — purchase, sale and trading of foreign currencies inside the Kingdom, and import/export of currencies for company/foreign-branch money changers; money transfers inside and outside the Kingdom permitted only to holders of valid SAMA licences at issuance (no new branches for that activity). Scope specific to money-exchange licensees; not general limits on bank customers · primary
  • Implementing Regulation to the Anti-Money Laundering Law — Article 23/1 (amended traveller declaration: currency, bearer negotiable instruments, gold bullion, precious metals, gemstones and worked jewellery reaching or exceeding SAR 40,000 or equivalent, entry and exit, written declaration under the prescribed form), Umm Al-Qura official gazette · primary
