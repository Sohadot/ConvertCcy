Exchange controls

Current international transactions: The IMF 2026 Informational Annex describes Switzerland's exchange system as free of multiple currency practices and of restrictions on payments and transfers for current international transactions, except security-related restrictions notified pursuant to Executive Board Decision No. 144–(52/51).
Security and sanctions: The Embargo Act is the Swiss framework law for sanctions implementation; concrete measures are contained in separate ordinances, and the Federal Council may enact measures such as financial sanctions, trade restrictions, travel bans and asset freezes.
Capital and investment reservations: OECD Code Annex B records actor- and transaction-specific Swiss reservations, including specified inward direct-investment situations, non-resident real-estate acquisition controls, and private pension-fund / insurance limits on foreign securities, money-market instruments and deposits with non-resident financial institutions.
Classification boundary: SNB monetary-policy intervention, Swiss border-cash controls, AML/KYC account-relationship duties, VAT conversion mechanics and SIC payment infrastructure are not classified by the governed Switzerland rule as exchange controls.

For travellers

Carrying cash in or out

Covered notes and coins, foreign currencies and bearer negotiable instruments (shares, bonds and cheques) may be carried into, through or out of Switzerland with no quantitative carriage ceiling under the FOCBS border-cash framework, and there is no spontaneous FOCBS declaration obligation merely for crossing the border. If questioned in a customs check, truthful information must be given; at CHF 10,000 or more, express questioning covers identity, origin, intended use and beneficial owner, and an entry is registered in the FOCBS information system. Below that amount, money-laundering or terrorist-financing suspicion can still trigger information powers, and provisional seizure may occur on suspicion.

  • Bringing currency in: On entry into Switzerland, covered notes and coins, foreign currencies and bearer negotiable instruments (shares, bonds and cheques) may be brought in with no quantitative inbound carriage ceiling under the FOCBS border-cash framework. Travellers are not obliged to make a spontaneous FOCBS declaration merely because covered cash crosses the inbound border. If FOCBS staff question a person about cash during an inbound check, truthful information must be provided. Where inbound cash amounts to at least CHF 10,000 or the foreign-currency equivalent, express questioning requires information on identity, the import of the cash, its origin, intended use and beneficial owner.
  • Taking currency out: On exit from Switzerland, the same FOCBS physical-carriage architecture applies to covered notes and coins, foreign currencies and bearer negotiable instruments leaving the country: there is no quantitative outbound carriage ceiling, and no spontaneous FOCBS declaration obligation for the outbound movement itself. This outbound field is limited to physical carriage of those instruments; it does not address salary remittance, business earnings or non-physical capital repatriation. If FOCBS staff question a person about cash during an outbound check, truthful information must be provided, and where outbound cash amounts to at least CHF 10,000 or the foreign-currency equivalent, express questioning covers identity, the export of the cash, origin, intended use and beneficial owner.
  • Border cash controls: There is no spontaneous FOCBS declaration threshold for covered notes, coins, foreign currencies or bearer negotiable instruments merely because they cross the Swiss border. Separately, where cash amounts to at least CHF 10,000 or the foreign-currency equivalent, express customs questioning requires information on identity, the cash movement, origin, intended use and beneficial owner, and an entry is registered in the FOCBS information system. Below CHF 10,000, money-laundering or terrorist-financing suspicion can still activate customs information powers. Provisional seizure under Customs Act Art. 104 is amount-independent and suspicion-based, and refused or false information on the identity and cash-movement limbs is an administrative offence — these are conduct mechanisms, not a numeric carriage ceiling or a declaration threshold.
Residency

Resident vs non-resident

  • Residents: The reviewed sources do not establish a statutory resident entitlement to open or hold a foreign-currency account. Where a financial intermediary nevertheless establishes or maintains a resident client relationship, AMLA requires verification of the contracting party's identity on a document of evidentiary value, and banks applying CDB 20 verify contracting-partner identity when opening accounts, passbooks or securities accounts. Beneficial owners must be identified and verified with due diligence appropriate to the circumstances, and banks require a beneficial-ownership statement at those openings. Doubt as to customer or beneficial-owner identity triggers repeated verification; records are checked and updated periodically according to the risk posed by the customer. Enhanced clarification of economic background and purpose applies only on conditional triggers — unusual relationships or transactions, relevant crime or terrorist-financing indications, higher risk, or specified AMLA data matches — not because the client is a resident. Boards of financial intermediaries set institutional money-laundering risk tolerance, including communications on particularly high-risk clients, high-risk countries and services outside that tolerance; for high-risk relationships of the kind addressed by FINMA, asset origin must be clarified in detail and verified as legal, with careful monitoring of higher-risk transactions. Successful KYC does not guarantee account acceptance.
  • Non-residents: The reviewed sources likewise do not establish a universal non-resident right to open or operate Swiss accounts, nor a universal ban. Foreign residence is not itself treated as automatic high risk and does not by itself force enhanced due diligence, source-of-wealth proof or rejection. Where an intermediary establishes a client relationship with a non-resident, the same AMLA identity, beneficial-owner identification/verification, doubt-triggered repetition and risk-based periodic updating duties apply, and banks under CDB 20 still verify contracting-partner identity and require beneficial-ownership statements when opening accounts or securities accounts. Enhanced clarification remains trigger-based — unusual activity, crime or terrorist-financing indications, higher-risk profiles such as high-risk countries or PEPs, or specified AMLA data matches — so country risk must not be collapsed into non-resident status. Institutional risk-tolerance decisions and FINMA high-risk-client origin-of-assets / monitoring expectations may still shape whether a particular non-resident relationship is accepted or escalated, without creating either a national entitlement or a national prohibition.
For business

Invoicing, settlement & conversion

Under Code of Obligations Art. 84, pecuniary debts are discharged in the legal tender of the currency in which the debt was incurred; a debt expressed in another currency may be discharged in the national currency of the place of payment at the maturity exchange rate unless the contract requires literal performance in 'actual currency' or equivalent wording. Separately, VAT classifies a consideration as foreign-currency where the invoice or receipt is issued in foreign currency, and foreign-currency considerations must be converted into Swiss francs for VAT assessment using FTA monthly average or daily sell rates. The Swiss Interbank Clearing (SIC) system is Switzerland's central payment infrastructure, and domestic account-to-account instant payments operate with immediate final settlement within their evidenced domestic scope.

  • Invoicing & settlement: Contractual debt denomination, invoice classification, VAT treatment, payment currency, accounting currency and payment rail are distinct. Under Code of Obligations Art. 84, pecuniary debts must be discharged in the legal tender of the currency in which the debt was incurred; a debt expressed other than in the national currency of the place of payment may be discharged in that national currency at the maturity exchange rate, unless the contract uses 'actual currency' or equivalent wording requiring literal performance in the stipulated currency. For VAT, a consideration is in foreign currency if the invoice or receipt is issued in foreign currency regardless of the payment currency, and foreign-currency considerations must be converted into Swiss francs for VAT assessment at the tax-claim date using the FTA monthly average rate or the daily exchange rate (sell). SIC is Switzerland's central payment system for eligible participants' large-value and related retail payments; domestic account-to-account instant payments provide immediate final settlement within their evidenced domestic scope.
  • Exchange controls: On current international transactions, the IMF 2026 Informational Annex describes Switzerland's exchange system as free of multiple currency practices and of restrictions on payments and transfers for current international transactions, except for security-related restrictions notified to the IMF pursuant to Executive Board Decision No. 144–(52/51). Separately, since 1 January 2003 the Embargo Act (Embargogesetz, EmbG) has been the framework law for Swiss sanctions implementation, with concrete measures in separate ordinances; under that Act the Federal Council may enact coercive measures such as financial sanctions, trade restrictions, travel bans or asset freezes. On capital and investment movements, OECD Code Annex B records actor- and transaction-specific Swiss reservations rather than a single global capital-account rule: List A I/A limits specified non-resident inward direct-investment sectors and situations; List B III subjects non-resident real-estate acquisition to cantonal authorisation where the property is not used for a permanent establishment, with holiday-home quotas and a bar on purely financial real-estate investments subject to exceptions; List A IV/D1 and List B V/D1 reserve private pension-fund and insurance purchases of specified non-resident-issued securities and money-market instruments above stated limits; and List B XI/B1–B2 reserves deposits of funds by private pension funds or insurance companies with non-resident financial institutions above the stated limits. SNB monetary-policy intervention capability, FOCBS border-cash controls, AML/KYC account-relationship duties, VAT conversion mechanics and SIC payment infrastructure are not classified here as exchange controls.
  • Banking & conversion: The Swiss Interbank Clearing (SIC) payment system is Switzerland's central payment system, operated by SIX Interbank Clearing Ltd on behalf of the Swiss National Bank for eligible participants' large-value and related retail payments. Domestic account-to-account instant payments allow private individuals and companies to settle with immediate execution and final settlement in seconds, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, within that domestic scope.
