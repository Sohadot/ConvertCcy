South Korea — Currency Passage Brief

For travellers

Carrying cash in or out

Travellers entering or leaving South Korea must file a mandatory declaration with the Korea Customs Service when their means of payment — foreign currency, Korean-won notes and checks combined — exceed USD 10,000 in total; the traveller marks item no. 3 of the Traveler Declaration Form and receives a Certificate of Foreign Currency Declaration. At or below USD 10,000 no declaration is required. This customs declaration is separate from the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act reporting that applies to capital transfers.

  • Bringing currency in: A traveller bringing means of payment into South Korea — foreign currency, Korean-won notes and checks combined — exceeding USD 10,000 in total must file a mandatory declaration with the Korea Customs Service, marking item no. 3 of the Traveler Declaration Form and receiving a Certificate of Foreign Currency Declaration. At or below USD 10,000 no permission or declaration is required. This customs declaration is distinct from any Foreign Exchange Transactions Act reporting on capital transactions.
  • Taking currency out: A traveller leaving South Korea must likewise file a mandatory Korea Customs declaration when means of payment exceed USD 10,000 in total. Separately, under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, capital-account transfers by residents may require transaction-specific notification or permission through a foreign exchange bank or the Bank of Korea Governor; that reporting is distinct from the customs border declaration.
  • Declaration: The Korea Customs declaration threshold is means of payment exceeding USD 10,000 in total — aggregating foreign currency, Korean-won notes and checks — for a traveller entering or leaving the country; it is a mandatory report to Customs (Traveler Declaration Form item no. 3, yielding a Certificate of Foreign Currency Declaration), not merely a document to produce if questioned, and it is triggered only above USD 10,000. This border declaration is separate from three other regimes: Foreign Exchange Transactions Act capital-transaction notification or permission, foreign exchange banks' reporting of FX sales over USD 10,000 to the National Tax Service, and KoFIU anti-money-laundering reporting.
Residency

Resident vs non-resident

  • Residents: South Korean residents may open and hold foreign-currency accounts — resident accounts and resident foreign currency trust accounts — through foreign exchange businesses registered under Article 8 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Capital transactions, which under Article 3 of the Act include deposits, lending, securities and overseas real estate, require advance reporting under FETA Article 18; certain methods of payment or receipt require reporting under Article 16; and some payments to foreign countries may require permission under Article 15. Depending on the transaction the report is made to a foreign exchange bank or the Bank of Korea Governor, and holding and transfer remain subject to sanctions, AML/KYC, tax and reporting requirements. This does not guarantee that any resident may open any particular account.
  • Non-residents: Non-residents may open and operate non-resident accounts through foreign exchange businesses registered under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act — external accounts and non-resident foreign currency trust accounts for foreign-currency funds, and non-resident Korean-won accounts and non-resident free-transfer Korean-won accounts for won funds (Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations Article 2-6-2). Certain transactions require transaction-specific reporting to a foreign exchange bank or the Bank of Korea Governor (for example FETA Article 18 capital transactions), and repatriation is not unconditional; account opening remains subject to sanctions screening, AML/KYC, tax and each institution's requirements. There is no entitlement to any particular account or service.
For business

Invoicing, settlement & conversion

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, current-account payments and receipts are substantially liberalised, while capital transactions (FETA Article 18) and certain methods of payment or receipt (FETA Article 16) require transaction-specific reporting, and some payments to foreign countries may require permission (FETA Article 15). The Minister of Economy and Finance (MOEF) is the competent authority under the Act; FETA Article 23 delegates specified powers to the Financial Services Commission, the Bank of Korea Governor, the Financial Supervisory Service Governor and foreign exchange agencies, and the actual filing or permission destination — a foreign exchange bank, the Bank of Korea Governor, or MOEF — depends on the transaction type and the applicable Act, Enforcement Decree and Regulations. Cross-border payments and foreign-currency accounts run through foreign exchange businesses registered under FETA Article 8. These FETA regimes are separate from the Korea Customs USD 10,000 border declaration, foreign exchange banks' reporting of foreign-currency sales over USD 10,000 to the National Tax Service (FETA Article 21), and KoFIU anti-money-laundering reporting.

  • Invoicing & settlement: Cross-border business payments and receipts run through foreign exchange businesses registered under Article 8 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Certain methods of payment or receipt require advance reporting under FETA Article 16 — settlement by offsetting a claim, settlement outside the period set by the Minister, payment to or from a person who is not a party to the transaction, and payment or receipt made without going through a foreign exchange agency — and capital transactions require reporting under Article 18, alongside sanctions, AML and reporting rules. This does not establish an unrestricted choice of invoice currency, which the Act does not govern and which depends on the contract and applicable rules.
  • Exchange controls: South Korea regulates foreign exchange through the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, administered by MOEF and the Bank of Korea. The Act sets procedures in three areas — transaction type, payment and receipt, and method of payment and receipt. Current-account payments and receipts are substantially liberalised, while capital transactions involving large fund movements are subject to transaction-specific notification or permission through foreign exchange banks, the Bank of Korea Governor, or MOEF. The Korean won is not fully internationalised. This is a regulated framework — not an absence of exchange controls and not full liberalisation of the capital account.
  • Banking & conversion: Currency conversion and cross-border payments in South Korea are provided through foreign exchange banks and other institutions authorised under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act within the Bank of Korea / MOEF framework. These authorised intermediaries apply the Act's notification, permission and reporting procedures according to transaction type; authorisation does not guarantee that any particular institution offers a given currency, service or price.
