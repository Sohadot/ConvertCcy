Brazil — Currency Passage Brief

BRL · Brazilian Real · Latin America

USD 10,000 (paper money in cash (national or foreign), excluding credit instruments, cheques and traveller's cheques; or the equivalent in another currency)

For travellers

Carrying cash in or out

Travellers may carry cash across Brazil's border, but amounts above USD 10,000 or the equivalent in another currency must be declared through the e-DBV. On departure, the traveller must present the declared cash and the applicable supporting evidence identified by Receita Federal.

  • Bringing currency in: The reviewed rules establish a declaration threshold rather than an explicit maximum carrying amount for travellers. Cash above USD 10,000 or the equivalent in another currency must be declared to Receita Federal through the e-DBV on entry.
  • Taking currency out: Cash above USD 10,000 or the equivalent in another currency must be declared through the e-DBV on departure. The traveller must present the declared cash and the applicable supporting evidence identified by Receita Federal, such as proof of purchase from an authorised FX institution, a prior entry declaration, or qualifying proof of receipt or withdrawal.
  • Declaration: Amounts above USD 10,000 or the equivalent in another currency must be declared to Receita Federal on entry and exit through the e-DBV. The reviewed customs guidance applies this requirement to paper money in cash, excluding credit instruments, checks, and traveller's checks.
Residency

Resident vs non-resident

  • Residents: Domestic foreign-currency accounts are available only under BCB-defined conditions and eligible account-holder categories. The reviewed sources do not establish unrestricted general access for all Brazilian residents.
  • Non-residents: The reviewed BCB framework governs foreign-currency accounts through defined eligible categories and operating conditions. The sources reviewed for this draft do not support a broad general statement that non-residents may operate such accounts 'more freely'; users should verify account eligibility under the current BCB rules applicable to their category.
For business

Invoicing, settlement & conversion

Foreign-exchange transactions generally must be conducted through institutions authorised by BCB, under the applicable regulatory and documentation framework.

  • Invoicing & settlement: FX market transactions must be conducted through institutions authorised by BCB. Transaction-specific settlement, documentation, and account arrangements should be verified under the applicable BCB regulatory framework and with the authorised institution handling the operation.
  • Exchange controls: Brazil operates a floating exchange-rate regime. BCB regulates the FX market, and FX transaction rates are freely agreed between authorised institutions and their clients.
  • Banking & conversion: Foreign-exchange conversion is available through institutions authorised by BCB to operate in the FX market, including banks and other authorised dealers identified in the current BCB framework.
