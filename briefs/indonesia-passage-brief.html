Indonesia — Currency Passage Brief

For travellers

Carrying cash in or out

A traveller carrying cash and/or bearer payment instruments (in Rupiah or foreign currency) whose total value reaches or exceeds IDR 100,000,000 (or the equivalent in another currency) must declare it to Indonesian Customs (Bea Cukai) on entry and on exit, through the applicable Customs Declaration channel (the All Indonesia integrated declaration for air and sea arrivals, and the Bea Cukai electronic Customs Declaration (e-CD) for land-border arrivals and for outbound cash declarations), under Government Regulation 99/2016. Separately, physically carrying Rupiah banknotes of IDR 100,000,000 or more out of Indonesia requires prior Bank Indonesia permission, whereas the same amount brought in is subject to a Customs authenticity examination; and carrying foreign banknotes (UKA) of IDR 1,000,000,000 or more is restricted to Bank Indonesia-authorised entities. These are customs / anti-money-laundering transparency and currency-carriage regimes, not a general cap on importing or exporting currency.

  • Bringing currency in: Cash and/or bearer payment instruments (cheques, traveller's cheques, promissory notes, bills of exchange), in Rupiah or foreign currency, with a total value reaching or exceeding IDR 100,000,000 (or the equivalent in another currency), must be declared to Indonesian Customs (Bea Cukai) on arrival via the applicable Customs Declaration channel (the All Indonesia integrated declaration for air and sea arrivals, and the Bea Cukai e-CD for land-border arrivals), under Government Regulation 99/2016. Where the cash brought in is Rupiah of IDR 100,000,000 or more, its authenticity is examined by Customs. Foreign banknotes (Uang Kertas Asing, UKA) with a value of IDR 1,000,000,000 or more may be carried into the customs area only by Bank Indonesia-authorised entities (banks and licensed non-bank money changers), not by ordinary individuals, under BI Regulation 19/7/PBI/2017 as amended. Below these thresholds the reviewed sources establish no declaration or permission requirement.
  • Taking currency out: On departure the same IDR 100,000,000 customs declaration duty applies to cash and bearer payment instruments in Rupiah or foreign currency (Government Regulation 99/2016), filed through the Bea Cukai electronic Customs Declaration (e-CD), which is the channel Bea Cukai designates for outbound cash declarations. Physically carrying Rupiah banknotes of IDR 100,000,000 or more out of Indonesia additionally requires prior Bank Indonesia permission, a licensing requirement distinct from and not symmetric with the inbound treatment (inbound Rupiah of that amount is instead subject to a Customs authenticity examination). Carrying foreign banknotes (UKA) of IDR 1,000,000,000 or more out of the customs area is restricted to Bank Indonesia-authorised entities (BI Regulation 19/7/PBI/2017 as amended). The reviewed sources do not establish a separate general cap on the amount of foreign currency an individual may take out beyond these declaration and carriage rules.
  • Declaration: The declaration threshold is a total value that reaches or exceeds IDR 100,000,000 (or the equivalent in another currency) in cash and/or bearer payment instruments, in Rupiah or foreign currency, for a traveller entering or leaving Indonesia (Government Regulation 99/2016), declared to Bea Cukai through the applicable Customs Declaration channel (the All Indonesia integrated declaration for air and sea arrivals; the Bea Cukai e-CD for land-border arrivals and for outbound cash declarations). Failure to declare carries an administrative fine of 10% of the amount carried, up to a maximum of IDR 300,000,000, to the extent established by the reviewed sources. This customs / anti-money-laundering border declaration is distinct from the Bank Indonesia Rupiah-carriage and foreign-banknote (UKA) carriage licensing regimes and from the DHE SDA export-proceeds regime, and it is not a currency import or export cap.
Residency

Resident vs non-resident

  • Residents: Residents (Penduduk) may hold and use foreign currency, while transactions conducted within Indonesian territory are generally required to be in Rupiah subject to statutory exceptions, including foreign-currency banking activities (BI Regulation 17/3/PBI/2015; statutory basis Law 7/2011 on Currency). The reviewed sources establish the Rupiah-use obligation and its exceptions but do not establish a general Bank Indonesia approval or quantitative limit on residents holding or operating foreign-currency accounts; the earlier on-file statement that residents face general BI limits or approval requirements for foreign-currency accounts is not supported by the located sources and is not asserted. Opening and operating bank accounts, including foreign-currency accounts, is governed by the general commercial-bank account-management rules (OJK Regulation POJK 24/2025) and by customer due diligence / know-your-customer under OJK Regulation POJK 8/2023; these govern the account-opening and account-administration process rather than establishing a residency-based entitlement. Under Indonesia's free-foreign-exchange framework (Law No. 24/1999), residents may hold and use foreign exchange, but the account-opening claim is not broadened beyond the located banking evidence, and account eligibility is not generalised beyond that evidence.
  • Non-residents: Non-residents (Bukan Penduduk) are subject to specific Bank Indonesia foreign-exchange-market restrictions in respect of Rupiah, including restrictions on Rupiah transfers abroad and on the provision of Rupiah credit or financing to non-residents, and limits on certain Rupiah transactions with non-residents (BI Regulation 18/19/PBI/2016; BI Regulation 24/7/PBI/2022 on the foreign-exchange market), subject to exceptions Bank Indonesia may allow. These are transaction- and market-level restrictions and do not by themselves establish a general non-resident bank-account-opening entitlement or prohibition. Account opening remains subject to the general commercial-bank account-management rules (OJK Regulation POJK 24/2025) and to customer due diligence / KYC (OJK Regulation POJK 8/2023). The located OJK sources prescribe customer-identification documentation, including foreign-citizen (Warga Negara Asing, WNA) documentation where applicable, but do not establish a general account-opening entitlement or prohibition based solely on Bank Indonesia resident/non-resident (Bukan Penduduk) status; foreign-citizen (WNA) status is a nationality concept and is not treated as synonymous with the BI non-resident (Bukan Penduduk) residency/economic-status concept. Account eligibility for non-residents therefore remains deliberately bounded and is not generalised beyond the located evidence. The earlier on-file claim that non-residents may transfer funds 'more freely' is not supported and is not asserted.
For business

Invoicing, settlement & conversion

Transactions conducted within Indonesian territory are generally required to use Rupiah, subject to statutory exceptions (including certain international-trade and international-financing transactions and foreign-currency banking activities) under BI Regulation 17/3/PBI/2015. Exporters of natural-resource products are separately subject to the DHE SDA regime (Government Regulation 36/2023 as amended through Government Regulation 21/2026, with Bank Indonesia's implementing rules), which requires export proceeds to be brought into and retained/placed within the Indonesian financial system for a minimum period; this is a sector-specific mandatory retention/placement obligation, not a general capital-repatriation approval requirement and not a rule applicable to all businesses. The reviewed sources do not establish an unrestricted right to invoice, settle or repatriate in any currency without condition.

  • Invoicing & settlement: Transactions conducted within Indonesian territory are generally required to use Rupiah, with statutory exceptions including certain international-trade (export/import) and international-financing transactions and foreign-currency bank activities (BI Regulation 17/3/PBI/2015; Law 7/2011 on Currency). Exporters of natural-resource products (DHE SDA) must bring their export proceeds into the Indonesian financial system and retain and place them for a minimum period under Government Regulation 36/2023 as amended by Government Regulations 8/2025, 2/2026 and 21/2026 and Bank Indonesia's implementing rules (BI Regulation 5/2026 and PADG 16/2026): non-oil-and-gas natural-resource proceeds are retained at 100% for at least 12 months, and oil-and-gas proceeds at least 30% for at least 3 months, placed in designated DHE special accounts at qualifying banks, with special flexibility for the mining sector under certain bilateral trade arrangements. This is a sector-specific mandatory bringing-in (pemasukan) and retention/placement (penempatan) obligation on export proceeds; it is not a general capital-repatriation approval requirement and does not apply to all Indonesian businesses. Other cross-border commercial settlement runs through authorised banks, and the reviewed sources do not establish an unrestricted right to invoice, settle or repatriate in any currency without condition.
  • Exchange controls: Indonesia does not operate a single general exchange-control authorisation regime in the reviewed sources; instead several distinct, layered regimes apply and must not be conflated: a customs declaration of cash and bearer instruments at or above IDR 100,000,000 (Government Regulation 99/2016); Bank Indonesia licensing and permission for physically carrying Rupiah banknotes (outbound of IDR 100,000,000 or more requires Bank Indonesia permission, while inbound of that amount is subject to a Customs authenticity examination) and foreign banknotes / UKA (IDR 1,000,000,000 or more restricted to authorised entities, BI Regulation 19/7/PBI/2017 as amended); the domestic Rupiah-use obligation with statutory exceptions (BI Regulation 17/3/PBI/2015); the DHE SDA export-proceeds retention and placement regime for natural-resource exporters (Government Regulation 36/2023 as amended through Government Regulation 21/2026); and foreign-exchange-market restrictions on Rupiah transactions with non-residents (BI Regulation 18/19/PBI/2016; BI Regulation 24/7/PBI/2022). The reviewed sources do not establish a general requirement of Bank Indonesia approval for capital transfers by residents, and they do not establish that Indonesia is 'fully liberalised' or has 'no controls'; absence of a located restriction is not evidence that none exists, and transaction-specific rules may apply.
  • Banking & conversion: Foreign-exchange conversion is available through banks authorised to conduct foreign-exchange activities and through licensed non-bank money changers (Kegiatan Usaha Penukaran Valuta Asing Bukan Bank, KUPVA Bukan Bank), which require a Bank Indonesia licence and are limited to authorised foreign-banknote exchange and related activities (BI Regulation 18/20/PBI/2016). Any conditions or limits Bank Indonesia applies specifically to money changers are particular to those licensees and are not general limits on bank customers. Documentation and underlying-transaction requirements may apply to larger foreign-exchange transactions, and the availability of a particular currency, service or rate is not guaranteed by any institution.

  • Bank Indonesia (BI) — central bank; monetary policy and the foreign-exchange framework · primary
  • Bank Indonesia — Monetary function: Flexible Inflation Targeting Framework (Flexible ITF); the Rupiah exchange rate floats (Bank Indonesia adopted a free-floating system in 1997), with Rupiah exchange-rate stability pursued as part of monetary policy — kept separate from capital controls, account eligibility, DHE SDA, currency declaration and physical-cash carriage · primary
  • Otoritas Jasa Keuangan (OJK) Regulation POJK No. 24 of 2025 — Account Management at Commercial Banks (Pengelolaan Rekening pada Bank Umum); the current general account-management / account-opening governance framework (standardised opening, closing, reactivation and account administration). Governs account management, not a residency-based account-opening entitlement or prohibition · primary
  • Otoritas Jasa Keuangan (OJK) Regulation POJK No. 8 of 2023 — Anti-Money-Laundering, Counter-Terrorism-Financing and Proliferation-Financing programme in the financial-services sector (APU-PPT); governs customer due diligence (CDD) / customer identification at bank account opening. Governs the CDD/KYC process, not a nationality-based account-opening entitlement or prohibition · primary
  • Law (Undang-Undang) No. 24 of 1999 — Free Foreign Exchange Flows and the Exchange Rate System (Lalu Lintas Devisa dan Sistem Nilai Tukar); the free-foreign-exchange framework under which residents may hold and use foreign exchange (does not by itself establish a bank account-opening entitlement) · primary
  • Directorate General of Customs and Excise (Bea Cukai / DJBC) — traveller cash and payment-instrument declaration; Customs Declaration / electronic Customs Declaration (e-CD) · primary
  • Indonesian Customs (Bea Cukai) — electronic Customs Declaration (e-CD) filing portal · primary
  • Government Regulation (Peraturan Pemerintah) No. 99 of 2016 — carrying cash and/or other payment instruments into or out of the Indonesian customs area (IDR 100,000,000 declaration; inbound Rupiah authenticity examination) · primary
