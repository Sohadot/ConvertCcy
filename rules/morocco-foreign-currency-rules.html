Morocco:
Foreign Currency Rules

Currency: MAD — Moroccan Dirham ISO: MA / MAR Last reviewed: 2026-05-01 Status: Reference · Informational

Country Overview

Morocco’s foreign-exchange framework is supervised by the Office des Changes, a public institution under the Ministry of Finance that regulates and controls foreign-exchange transactions and issues the applicable exchange regulations. The official materials reviewed for this entry show a system built around authorised intermediaries, regulated foreign-currency accounts, travel allocations for eligible residents, and documentary controls for certain foreign-banknote transactions. Morocco should therefore be understood as a governed exchange-regulation jurisdiction rather than a jurisdiction of unrestricted free use of foreign cash.

Rules at a Glance

Bringing foreign currency into the country
The Office des Changes account rules indicate that when foreign banknotes are deposited into eligible foreign-currency or convertible-dirham account structures, the depositing party may need to provide the original foreign-currency import declaration signed by customs at the border, dated no more than six months earlier, or another acceptable document such as an exchange receipt within the stated time conditions. This shows that bringing foreign cash into Morocco is not operationally detached from documentary control, especially where later banking use is involved. Travelers should therefore preserve entry-side documentation and use authorised channels.
Taking foreign currency out of the country
The official pages reviewed here focus more on authorised account operation and resident travel allocations than on a single public customs-cash export threshold. However, they clearly show that outward foreign-currency use is structured through authorised intermediaries, travel-allocation mechanisms, and account rules rather than treated as an unregulated cash matter. Users should therefore verify current exit-side cash and declaration practice directly with Moroccan Customs and their bank or exchange provider before departure.
Cash declaration threshold
The Office des Changes pages reviewed for this entry publicly confirm the operational importance of a customs import declaration for certain later uses of foreign banknotes, but they do not state a public numeric traveler threshold on the pages cited here. Accordingly, this entry does not insert an unsupported figure. For operational use, travelers should confirm the current customs declaration threshold directly with Moroccan Customs while also preserving any declaration or exchange documentation needed for later banking or reconversion purposes.
Rules for residents holding foreign currency
The official resident-focused pages reviewed here show that Morocco regulates resident access to foreign currency through defined exchange facilities rather than through an unrestricted open-cash model. For personal travel, eligible resident categories may benefit from the official personal-travel allocation and any supplementary amount within the stated framework. Residents should therefore treat foreign-currency access as a regulated entitlement and authorised-channel matter, not as a purely private unregulated cash issue.
Rules for non-residents
The Office des Changes states that banks may open foreign-currency accounts and convertible-dirham accounts in the name of foreign resident or non-resident natural persons, foreign legal persons and their representations in Morocco, as well as certain internationally oriented entities. This means non-residents have officially recognised foreign-currency account pathways inside the Moroccan system. At the same time, account operation remains subject to the formal rules and documentary expectations set by the exchange-regulation framework.
Business invoicing and settlement in foreign currency
The official materials reviewed for this entry show that Moroccan business-related foreign-currency use operates through regulated account structures, authorised intermediaries, and explicit travel-allocation mechanisms for business travel. They do not support a characterization of Morocco as a free-form foreign-currency settlement environment. Businesses should therefore treat foreign-currency invoicing, settlement, and account use as matters to be structured through authorised banks and within the exchange regulations administered by the Office des Changes.
Exchange controls and approval requirements
The Office des Changes explicitly states that it regulates and controls foreign-exchange transactions, draws up exchange regulations, ensures compliance with applicable exchange rules, and may grant exemptions or approvals where appropriate. This is the clearest official indication that Morocco operates a real exchange-control framework. Users should therefore expect foreign-currency operations to be rule-based, channel-based, and document-sensitive.
Banking and conversion practicality
The official Moroccan framework channels practical foreign-currency use through banks, authorised exchange traders, and payment institutions acting as authorised intermediaries. The recent Office des Changes materials on the supplementary tourist allocation also show that activation of rights takes place at approved exchange points. Practically, users should convert and activate foreign-exchange rights only through authorised operators and should keep receipts and related documents for later verification.

Practical Summaries

Traveler summary

The Office des Changes publicly states that Moroccan resident individuals, Moroccans residing abroad, and resident foreigners may benefit from a personal-travel allocation, with a base amount of 100,000 dirhams that may be increased within the official cap through the supplementary tourist-allocation process. The official account rules also show that where foreign banknotes are later deposited into eligible foreign-currency structures, supporting customs import-declaration documentation may be required. In practical terms, travelers should use authorised exchange points, keep exchange documentation, and not assume that undocumented foreign-banknote movement can later be regularised effortlessly.

Business summary

For business users, the official framework reviewed here shows that Morocco uses authorised intermediaries and regulated account structures for foreign-currency operations. The Office des Changes allows specific foreign-currency or convertible-dirham account structures for non-residents and for certain internationally oriented entities, and it also provides regulated business-travel-allocation mechanisms. Businesses should therefore approach foreign-currency use in Morocco through authorised banking and exchange channels and within the formal exchange-regulation framework.
