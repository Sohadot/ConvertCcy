Italy:
Foreign Currency Rules

Currency: EUR — Euro ISO: IT / ITA Last reviewed: 2026-07-24 Lifecycle: published Indexing: allowed

Country Overview

Italy is a euro-area member of the European Union. Cross-border movements of cash are administered by the Agenzia delle Dogane e dei Monopoli (ADM), the Italian customs authority, together with the Guardia di Finanza, under Regulation (EU) 2018/1672 for cash entering or leaving the European Union and under Legislative Decree 195/2008 (Article 3) for cash entering or leaving Italian national territory, including movements to and from other EU Member States. The euro is legal tender and Italy applies no national exchange-control approval regime; within the EU legal framework the free movement of capital is guaranteed under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, subject to Treaty exceptions and to sanctions, anti-money-laundering, tax, reporting and prudential controls rather than exchange controls. Banking, payment and conversion services are provided by intermediaries authorised and supervised by Banca d'Italia within the Eurosystem.

Rules at a Glance

Bringing foreign currency into the country
Cash of EUR 10,000 or more brought into Italy must be declared to the Italian customs authority (ADM) at the first border customs office. Where the traveller enters the European Union through Italy the obligation arises under Article 3 of Regulation (EU) 2018/1672; where the traveller enters Italian national territory from another EU Member State it arises under Article 3 of Legislative Decree 195/2008. 'Cash' includes currency, bearer-negotiable instruments and gold used as a highly liquid store of value.
Taking foreign currency out of the country
Cash of EUR 10,000 or more taken out of Italy must be declared to the Italian customs authority (ADM) at the first border customs office. The obligation applies on leaving the European Union through Italy (Article 3, Regulation (EU) 2018/1672) and on leaving Italian national territory towards another EU Member State (Article 3, Legislative Decree 195/2008). The same cash categories apply; a declaration with inexact or incomplete information does not satisfy the obligation.
Cash declaration threshold
The threshold for a mandatory declaration of accompanied cash carried by a traveller is EUR 10,000 or more. Two parallel regimes apply and are administered through a single ADM declaration form: Article 3 of Regulation (EU) 2018/1672 for cash entering or leaving the European Union, and Article 3 of Legislative Decree 195/2008 for cash entering or leaving Italian national territory (including to and from other EU Member States). Unaccompanied cash sent by post, freight or courier is covered separately by Article 4 of Regulation (EU) 2018/1672 (disclosure on the request of customs). Customs may require production of the cash and apply administrative seizure; specific penalty amounts are fixed by Legislative Decree 195/2008.
Rules for residents holding foreign currency
Within the EU Treaty framework, general restrictions on capital movements and payments are prohibited (Article 63 TFEU), and no Italy-specific general prior-approval exchange-control regime applies to residents holding or transferring foreign currency. This does not guarantee that any given resident may open any particular foreign-currency account: holding and transferring funds remain subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering and identity verification, taxation, reporting, prudential rules (Article 65 TFEU), and each supervised intermediary's own conditions.
Rules for non-residents
Article 63 TFEU prohibits restrictions on capital movements and payments both between Member States and with third countries, so no general exchange-control restriction applies to non-residents holding accounts or transferring funds in Italy. This creates no entitlement to an account or service: onboarding, taxation, sanctions screening, anti-money-laundering and identity verification, reporting, and each institution's conditions apply, and the Treaty preserves certain third-country exceptions (Articles 64 and 65 TFEU).
Business invoicing and settlement in foreign currency
Cross-border business payments and settlement are not subject to a general exchange-control approval regime; under Article 63 TFEU payments between Member States and with third countries are free of restriction, subject to the Treaty's exceptions and to sanctions, anti-money-laundering, tax and reporting rules. Such payments are routed through banks, payment institutions and electronic-money institutions authorised and supervised by Banca d'Italia. This framework does not by itself establish an unrestricted choice of invoice currency, which depends on the contract and applicable rules.
Exchange controls and approval requirements
Italy maintains no national exchange-control regime for capital movements or payments. Under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, all restrictions on the movement of capital and on payments, between Member States and with third countries, are prohibited. This is not unconditional: the Treaty preserves exceptions, including measures on taxation and the prudential supervision of financial services and declaration procedures for administrative or statistical purposes (Article 65), the option to maintain certain restrictions on direct investment involving third countries (Article 64), and temporary safeguard measures (Article 66), alongside EU restrictive measures (sanctions). This capital-movement framework is separate from the EUR 10,000 cross-border cash-declaration regimes.
Banking and conversion practicality
Currency conversion and cross-border payment services in Italy are provided through banks, payment institutions and electronic-money institutions authorised and supervised by Banca d'Italia, which maintains the supervisory registers of these intermediaries. Authorisation and supervision establish the regulatory routing; they do not guarantee that any particular institution offers a given currency, service or price. Access remains subject to each intermediary's onboarding, anti-money-laundering and identity verification, sanctions screening, and reporting requirements.

Practical Summaries

Traveler summary

Travellers carrying cash of EUR 10,000 or more must lodge a declaration with the Italian customs authority (ADM) at the first border customs office. The obligation applies both to cash entering or leaving the European Union (Article 3, Regulation (EU) 2018/1672) and to cash entering or leaving Italian national territory, including movements to or from other EU Member States (Article 3, Legislative Decree 195/2008). 'Cash' covers currency (banknotes and coins), bearer-negotiable instruments, and goods used as highly liquid stores of value such as gold.

Business summary

Italy applies no national exchange-control regime: under Article 63 TFEU restrictions on capital movements and payments are prohibited, subject to Treaty exceptions (taxation, prudential supervision, declaration procedures, certain third-country measures and safeguards) and to sanctions, anti-money-laundering, tax and reporting rules. Cross-border payments, foreign-currency accounts and conversion are routed through banks, payment institutions and electronic-money institutions authorised and supervised by Banca d'Italia, without any guarantee of a specific account or service. The EUR 10,000 cross-border cash-declaration regimes are separate from this capital-movement framework.
