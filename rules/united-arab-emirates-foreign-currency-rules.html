United Arab Emirates:
Foreign Currency Rules

Currency: AED — UAE Dirham ISO: AE / ARE Last reviewed: 2026-05-01 Lifecycle: published Indexing: allowed

Country Overview

The United Arab Emirates applies a convertible dirham pegged to the US dollar alongside a supervisory framework centred on the Central Bank of the UAE (CBUAE) for licensed banking and regulated exchange-business activity. Customs and traveller transparency measures complement that framework rather than imposing retail exchange quotas: traveller declarations focus on bearer instruments, cash, jewellery, and precious metals above published thresholds rather than banning foreign currency outright. Interpreted together, the Emirates present as a predominantly open-account environment with AML/CFT and customs reporting obligations layered on cross-border carriage and financial-institution workflows.

Rules at a Glance

Bringing foreign currency into the country
Inbound travellers may carry foreign currency without a legislated statutory cap analogous to rigid import licences. When the traveller carries currencies, cheque-like bearer forms, jewellery, or precious metals whose combined declarable valuation exceeds AED 60,000, UAE customs doctrine expects a declaration aligned with traveller-transparency publications and ICP-aligned digital facilitation at covered ports. Household members under eighteen are not ordinarily required to lodge a standalone declaration beyond inclusion on a guardian declaration where facts support that treatment. Omitting mandated declarations where threshold facts exist is prosecuted as customs evasion risk rather than a routine paperwork preference.
Taking foreign currency out of the country
Departure differs operationally from arrival even when headline policy remains permissive: travellers leaving still face the AED 60,000 combined declarable corridor for cash-like and jewellery categories, but the supervisory emphasis shifts to outbound reporting and AML monitoring on later bank or wire corridors. Capital repatriation of salaries, divestment receipts, or investment proceeds is ordinarily not withheld by denomination-specific quotas; compliance teams instead scrutinise sourcing and screening on higher-value corridors. Institutional channels therefore remain the pragmatic choice for sizeable movements that would otherwise collide with traveller-only declaration mechanics.
Cash declaration threshold
Federal traveller declaration standards align on a combined AED 60,000 reporting trigger across cash, negotiable instruments without a named payee, jewellery, and precious metals for passengers aged eighteen and above. The measure is grounded in anti-money-laundering transparency rather than a permit to carry only up to that mark. Officials provide structured declaration forms plus, on selected crossings, interoperable electronic submission consistent with ICA/ICP public guidance. Accuracy matters because valuation disputes feed enforcement channels separate from benign tourist exchanges below reporting facts.
Rules for residents holding foreign currency
UAE residents maintain AED and multicurrency deposits domestically subject to universal banking AML standards rather than nationality-based holding prescriptions. Everyday conversion between dirhams and majors occurs through authorised institutions where CBUAE licence classes govern who may run exchange counters versus full banking. Residents remitting abroad use SWIFT-like rails with institution-level checks; there is no parallel household foreign-currency ceiling published like hard exchange-control jurisdictions. Relationship managers still expect documentation on unusually large legacy cash deposits.
Rules for non-residents
Non-resident individuals and corporates open AED or foreign-currency accounts with documentation typical of international banking centres. Repatriation of operating profits, sale proceeds, or payroll savings is not administratively throttled by sovereign conversion permits in the classic sense, though banks apply sanctions screening and correspondent-bank constraints. Non-residents engaging in mainland commercial activity coordinate with licensing authorities separately from FX mechanics. Digital wallets and PSP partnerships expand retail access but inherit the same compliance obligations.
Business invoicing and settlement in foreign currency
UAE entities invoice cross-border counterparties in foreign currency without coercion to forcibly surrender proceeds into AED at a punitive spread. Settlement may therefore remain offshore or route through correspondent nostros as commercial contracts dictate. Any firm selling exchange as a standalone product must hold Central Bank approvals under prevailing exchange-business regulation tiers rather than operating as an unregulated bureau. Consolidated groups still centralise treasury under policies that AML teams audit irrespective of denomination choice.
Exchange controls and approval requirements
Administrative parlance distinguishes true exchange controls—mandatory approvals per transaction—from the Emirates' supervisory posture, which concentrates on peg maintenance through monetary instruments and regulated institutions while keeping current-account style flows largely liberal. Profit repatriation, services exports, and re-invoicing frameworks do not require pre-clearance dossiers analogous to guarded-capital regimes. Where tension appears, it is typically compliance-driven delays at bank level rather than sovereign denial of lawful conversion.
Banking and conversion practicality
Liquidity for tourists and corporates flows through multinational banks, local champions, licensed exchange houses, and airport concessions with published boards. Payments rails include instant domestic transfers where supported and cross-border wires with correspondent cut-offs. Corporates leaning on treasury workstations integrate FX hedging externally while domestic spot needs are satisfied OTC. Operational friction concentrates on onboarding due diligence—not on statutory quotas—when aligning with FATF-aligned programmes.

Practical Summaries

Traveler summary

Visitors and residents may move foreign currency broadly freely, subject to traveller declaration rules when declared items exceed AED 60,000 in combined value across cash, bearer instruments, jewellery, and precious metals, using approved customs procedures (including designated digital declaration channels where applicable).

Business summary

Business invoicing and bank transfers are unconstrained by classic exchange-control ceilings in the sovereign sense used elsewhere; AML/CFT and licensing rules still govern intermediaries trading currency and moving funds at scale.
