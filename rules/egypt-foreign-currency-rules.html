Egypt:
Foreign Currency Rules

Currency: EGP — Egyptian Pound ISO: EG / EGY Last reviewed: 2026-09-08 Lifecycle: published Indexing: allowed

Country Overview

Egypt's currency is the Egyptian pound (EGP). Under Law No. 194 of 2020 Art. 211 (Official Gazette / NIB PDF p. 106), the Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) Board issues the rules governing the foreign-exchange market, and the EGP exchange rate against foreign currencies is determined by the interaction of supply and demand in that market — a market-determined exchange-rate framework kept analytically separate from FX-channel regulation. The CBE Special MPC of 6 March 2024 stated that the exchange rate is allowed to be determined by market forces while the CBE continues transitioning toward a flexible inflation-targeting regime; current 2026 CBE Monetary Policy Report and live market/CBE exchange-rate pages treat the market-determined rate as operative. Cross-border FX operations are regulated and must be conducted through banks or other entities licensed under Law 194, subject to Board rules (Art. 212) — not a claim of unrestricted convertibility of every source of funds. Traveller cash is dual-layered: Law 194 Art. 213 governs foreign-currency physical carriage and Art. 213-form disclosure for نقد أجنبي, while AML Law 80/2002 Art. 12 and Executive Regulation Art. 14 add a distinct customs-disclosure layer for foreign currency and bearer negotiable instruments (including a lower outbound AML disclosure trigger). Physical EGP banknote carriage is a separate Board-set ceiling under Decision 2200/2020. Absence of a located restriction is not evidence that none exists.

Rules at a Glance

Bringing foreign currency into the country
Law 194/2020 Art. 213 (Official Gazette / NIB PDF p. 106) provides that bringing foreign currency into Egypt is guaranteed for all arrivals. There is no general quantitative inbound import ceiling in Art. 213. Disclosure on the prescribed form is required when the amount exceeds USD 10,000 or its equivalent in other foreign currencies (Arabic: إذا جاوز عشرة آلاف دولار — strictly 'exceeds' / 'جاوز', not '>=' / 'USD 10,000 or more'). Exactly USD 10,000 is outside this Art. 213 inbound disclosure trigger. This Art. 213 inbound limb addresses foreign currency (نقد أجنبي) only. Separately, AML Law 80/2002 Art. 12 and Exec Reg Art. 14(3) require customs disclosure when the value of foreign currency, bearer negotiable instruments, or both combined exceeds USD 10,000 equivalent (تجاوز / >) — an AML customs-disclosure layer, not an Art. 213 import ceiling and not a reason to fold BNI into Art. 213.
Taking foreign currency out of the country
Outbound foreign-currency cash under Law 194/2020 Art. 213 (Official Gazette / NIB PDF pp. 106–107) is not symmetric with inbound. Ordinary departure rule: foreign currency may be taken out provided it does not exceed USD 10,000 or equivalent (Arabic: بشرط ألا يزيد على عشرة آلاف دولار — ordinary physical-carriage ceiling, operator <=). Separate exception: at departure, travellers may carry what remains of amounts previously disclosed upon arrival even where that remainder exceeds USD 10,000 (Art. 213 applies this remainder to travellers generally for FX). Separately, AML Exec Reg Art. 14(4) requires outbound customs disclosure when foreign currency, BNI, or both combined exceed USD 5,000 equivalent — a disclosure threshold distinct from the Art. 213 FX carriage ceiling, expressly without prejudice to the banking-law carriage architecture. Do not publish a symmetric 'USD 10,000 declaration at entry and exit'. Postal conveyance of Egyptian or foreign cash by letters/parcels is prohibited under Art. 213 and Exec Reg Art. 14(6). Physical EGP banknotes remain a separate Board-limit regime (Decision 2200/2020).
Cash declaration threshold
Keep layers separate. (A) Law 194 Art. 213 FX cash: inbound disclosure when FX exceeds USD 10,000 (جاوز / >); no general inbound FX ceiling; outbound ordinary FX carriage <= USD 10,000 plus previously-disclosed inbound remainder exception. (B) AML Law 80 Art. 12 / Exec Reg Art. 14 customs disclosure for FX and/or BNI (aggregated): inbound disclosure when value exceeds USD 10,000; outbound disclosure when value exceeds USD 5,000 — outbound disclosure threshold ≠ outbound Art. 213 FX carriage ceiling. (C) EGP physical banknotes: Decision 2200/2020 Art. 1 — carriage within five thousand EGP entering or leaving (carriage ceiling, not a declaration threshold); Exec Reg Art. 14(5) also caps EGP banknotes or BNI at five thousand EGP. (D) BNI: defined in Exec Reg Art. 1; entry/exit treatment under Art. 12 / Art. 14 — not Art. 213 FX semantics. Do not collapse these into one symmetric USD 10,000 entry/exit figure.
Rules for residents holding foreign currency
Law 194/2020 Art. 212 (Official Gazette / NIB PDF p. 106) establishes that every natural or legal person may retain, own or possess foreign currency accruing to, owned by or held by that person, and may conduct foreign-exchange operations including inward and outward transfers, through banks or other entities licensed under the Law, according to rules and procedures set by the CBE Board. Pinpoint CBE currentness sweep located no residency-specific overlay that restores a general resident FX-holding authorisation requirement. Art. 212 is an eligibility / channel architecture, not a guaranteed entitlement to every bank product or account type, and not permission to deal outside licensed channels. Domestic transactions inside Egypt must be in EGP unless an international agreement, another law, or a Board-authorised case provides otherwise — a domestic settlement rule kept separate from international transfer rights.
Rules for non-residents
Art. 212 is framed for every natural or legal person. Pinpoint CBE currentness sweep located no primary residency-divergence overlay establishing a general freer non-resident convertibility baseline. Non-resident and resident treatment therefore start from the same Art. 212 licensed-channel baseline. Product/account-specific non-resident categories remain transaction-specific and are not asserted as a general freer convertibility right. Note: AML Exec Reg Art. 14(2)'s remainder limb is drafted for non-Egyptians on the AML carriage overlay; Law 194 Art. 213's previously-disclosed FX remainder applies to travellers generally and is the banking-system FX rule.
Business invoicing and settlement in foreign currency
Cross-border FX settlement follows Art. 212: operations including inward/outward transfers through banks or other licensed entities under Board rules — not bank-only. Art. 214 (Official Gazette / NIB PDF p. 107) authorises banks to conduct FX operations (deposits, dealing, inward/outward transfers, deployment/cover); banks may import/export foreign currencies with CBE approval. Art. 215 requires banks, exchange companies and other licensed FX entities to report FX operations to the CBE, which supervises implementation. Domestic Egyptian transactions default to EGP under Art. 212 unless a legal exception applies. A 2024–2026 Foreign Currency Activities / related circular currentness sweep through execution date did not locate a post–6 March 2024 instrument re-imposing shortage-era general FX-access denial or a universal bank-only eligibility rule; operational reporting requirements are distinguished from legal eligibility. Absence of a located transaction-specific restriction is not evidence that none exists.
Exchange controls and approval requirements
Egypt's current architecture is not usefully summarised as legacy 'managed float with resident capital controls / largely liberalised current account'. The statute and current CBE monetary evidence establish: (i) market-determined EGP rate by supply and demand under Board FX-market rules (Art. 211), reinforced by the 6 March 2024 Special MPC market-forces decision and current 2026 monetary reporting; (ii) a broad Art. 212 right to retain FX and transfer inward/outward through licensed channels under Board procedures; (iii) a domestic EGP settlement default; (iv) dual traveller-cash layers — Art. 213 FX carriage/disclosure plus AML Art. 12 / Exec Reg Art. 14 FX+BNI customs disclosure (including outbound AML disclosure > USD 5,000); and (v) Arts 214–215 licensed multi-channel FX market supervision. On existing Passage Check taxonomy the exchange-control posture is floating_regulated_market: a market-determined exchange-rate framework with FX retention and inward/outward transfers through licensed channels, while domestic EGP settlement rules, licensed-market supervision and separate AML/cross-border cash controls remain operative — not a free float, not 'no exchange controls', and not a claim of fully liberalised or unregulated convertibility.
Banking and conversion practicality
Foreign-exchange conversion for the public is available through CBE-supervised banks and through exchange companies and other entities licensed to deal in foreign exchange under Law 194/2020 (Arts 212, 214–215; CBE On-Site Supervision page). FX conversion is therefore a licensed multi-channel activity, not bank-only and not an unregulated free-for-all. Availability of a particular currency, rate or product remains subject to licence scope, Board rules, documentary requirements and commercial terms.

Practical Summaries

Traveler summary

Dual architecture — do not collapse. Law 194/2020 Art. 213 (FX cash only): inbound foreign currency free with no general quantitative ceiling; Art. 213-form disclosure when the amount exceeds USD 10,000 equivalent (جاوز / strictly >; exactly USD 10,000 outside this inbound trigger); outbound ordinary FX carriage not exceeding USD 10,000 equivalent (ألا يزيد على / <=); travellers may also carry the remainder of FX previously disclosed on arrival even where that remainder exceeds USD 10,000. Separately, AML Law 80/2002 Art. 12 / Exec Reg Art. 14 require customs disclosure of foreign currency and/or bearer negotiable instruments (or both combined): inbound when value exceeds USD 10,000; outbound when value exceeds USD 5,000 — the outbound AML disclosure threshold is distinct from and lower than the Art. 213 FX carriage ceiling. Physical EGP banknotes: Decision 2200/2020 Art. 1 sets a five-thousand-EGP carriage ceiling for persons entering or leaving; Exec Reg Art. 14(5) also limits EGP banknotes or BNI to five thousand EGP. Postal conveyance of Egyptian or foreign cash is prohibited.

Business summary

Law 194/2020 Art. 212 permits natural and legal persons to conduct FX operations, including inward and outward transfers, through banks or other licensed entities under Board rules and procedures. Domestic transactions inside Egypt are in EGP unless an international agreement, another law, or a Board-authorised case provides otherwise. Arts 214–215 and CBE supervision establish a licensed multi-channel FX market (banks, exchange companies and other licensed FX entities), not a bank-only monopoly. This is not a claim that every cross-border payment is unrestricted.
