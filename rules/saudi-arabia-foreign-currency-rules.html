Saudi Arabia:
Foreign Currency Rules

Currency: SAR — Saudi Riyal ISO: SA / SAU Last reviewed: 2026-09-06 Lifecycle: published Indexing: allowed

Country Overview

Saudi Arabia's monetary regime is administered by the Saudi Central Bank (SAMA), which maintains a fixed exchange rate for the Saudi Riyal against the US Dollar (SAR 3.75 per USD). Foreign exchange is provided through licensed banks and SAMA-licensed money-exchange businesses. Bank-account opening is governed by SAMA's Account Opening Rules, which set eligibility conditions by customer category, and travellers crossing the border must declare currency and specified valuables at or above a threshold set under the Anti-Money Laundering Law's Implementing Regulation. The foreign-exchange market is licensed and regulated by SAMA. The reviewed official sources do not establish a general regime of exchange controls or a currency-authorisation requirement; absence of a located restriction is not evidence that none exists. The monetary peg, the licensed foreign-exchange market, account-eligibility rules, the cross-border declaration and AML requirements are distinct regimes and are not conflated.

Rules at a Glance

Bringing foreign currency into the country
A traveller entering Saudi Arabia must declare to ZATCA any currency (Saudi or foreign), bearer negotiable instruments, gold bullion, precious metals, gemstones or worked jewellery whose total value reaches or exceeds SAR 40,000 or the equivalent in another currency, using the prescribed declaration mechanism. Below that threshold no declaration is required. The declaration duty derives from Article 23/1 of the Implementing Regulation to the Anti-Money Laundering Law.
Taking foreign currency out of the country
A traveller leaving Saudi Arabia must likewise declare currency, bearer negotiable instruments, gold bullion, precious metals, gemstones or worked jewellery whose total value reaches or exceeds SAR 40,000 or the equivalent (Article 23/1 of the Implementing Regulation to the Anti-Money Laundering Law); the obligation is symmetric with entry. The reviewed official sources do not establish a separate Saudi Central Bank permission or export cap on physically carrying currency out beyond this declaration duty.
Cash declaration threshold
The declaration threshold is a total value reaching or exceeding SAR 40,000 (or the equivalent in another currency) in currency, bearer negotiable instruments, gold bullion, precious metals, gemstones or worked jewellery, for a traveller entering or leaving the Kingdom (Article 23/1 of the Implementing Regulation to the Anti-Money Laundering Law), declared to ZATCA through the prescribed mechanism. This anti-money-laundering border declaration is distinct from bank AML/KYC reporting and from SAMA's account-eligibility rules.
Rules for residents holding foreign currency
Under SAMA's Account Opening Rules, bank customers may open accounts in any available foreign currency and deposit and withdraw in that currency; where the currency is unavailable, payment may be made in Saudi riyal. Eligibility to open and operate an account depends on the account-opening conditions for the customer's category and on AML/KYC requirements; the reviewed sources do not establish an unconditional right for any person to hold a particular account.
Rules for non-residents
Under SAMA's Account Opening Rules, banks may not open a Saudi-riyal account, a foreign-currency account, or any other account for non-Saudi, non-GCC natural persons who do not reside in Saudi Arabia unless written approval from the Ministry of Interior or the Ministry of Foreign Affairs is obtained through SAMA. Saudi citizens residing abroad and GCC nationals are addressed by their own account-opening rules within the same framework. The reviewed sources therefore do not support a general statement that non-residents may open or operate accounts freely; eligibility is category-specific and, for non-Saudi/non-GCC non-residents, conditional on the prescribed approval.
Business invoicing and settlement in foreign currency
Cross-border business payments and currency conversion run through licensed banks and SAMA-licensed money-exchange businesses, subject to AML/KYC and SAMA's account-opening rules. The reviewed official sources support ordinary commercial settlement through these licensed institutions but do not establish an unrestricted right to invoice, settle or repatriate in any currency without condition; invoice-currency and repatriation arrangements should be verified for the specific transaction.
Exchange controls and approval requirements
The Saudi Riyal is maintained at a fixed exchange rate against the US Dollar (SAR 3.75) by the Saudi Central Bank (SAMA); this is a monetary-regime fact and does not by itself establish the presence or absence of exchange or capital-movement controls. The reviewed official sources establish a licensed foreign-exchange market (banks and SAMA-licensed money-exchange businesses), account-eligibility conditions by customer category, a cross-border AML declaration regime and AML/KYC requirements; they do not establish a general exchange-control authorisation regime for buying, holding or transferring currency. Absence of a located restriction is not evidence that none exists, and narrower sectoral or transaction-specific rules may apply.
Banking and conversion practicality
Currency conversion and transfers are available through licensed banks and SAMA-licensed money-exchange businesses, within their respective licensed scope. Any client conditions or limits that SAMA applies to money-exchange businesses are specific to those licensees and are not general limits on bank customers. Availability of a particular currency, service or price is not guaranteed by any institution.

Practical Summaries

Traveler summary

Travellers entering or leaving Saudi Arabia must submit a declaration to the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority (ZATCA) when the currency, bearer negotiable instruments, gold bullion, precious metals, gemstones or worked jewellery they carry reach or exceed SAR 40,000 (or the equivalent in another currency) in total. The obligation is symmetric on entry and exit and is filed through the prescribed ZATCA declaration mechanism.

Business summary

Foreign-currency accounts and conversion are available through licensed banks and SAMA-licensed money-exchange businesses, subject to SAMA's Account Opening Rules and to AML/KYC requirements. The reviewed official sources establish account-eligibility conditions by customer category rather than an unrestricted right to hold or transfer currency; ordinary commercial settlement through licensed institutions is supported, while unrestricted capital repatriation is not established by the reviewed sources.
