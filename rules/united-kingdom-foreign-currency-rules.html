United Kingdom:
Foreign Currency Rules

Currency: GBP — British Pound Sterling ISO: GB / GBR Last reviewed: 2026-07-08 Lifecycle: published Indexing: allowed

Country Overview

The United Kingdom operates a fully liberalised foreign-exchange regime with no general exchange controls since 1979. The framework is governed by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) and HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC). There are no restrictions on current or capital account transactions for residents or non-residents.

Rules at a Glance

Bringing foreign currency into the country
Foreign currency and sterling may be freely imported. Declaration to Border Force is required when carrying cash of £10,000 or more into Great Britain, or €10,000 or more into Northern Ireland from a non-EU country outside the UK, or the equivalent in foreign currency. An advance declaration may be submitted online up to 72 hours before travel; declaration on arrival is also permitted.
Taking foreign currency out of the country
Residents and non-residents may freely export foreign currency and sterling. Declaration to Border Force is required when taking out cash of £10,000 or more from Great Britain, or €10,000 or more from Northern Ireland to a non-EU country outside the UK, or the equivalent in foreign currency. Non-residents may repatriate the full amount of currency declared on entry.
Cash declaration threshold
Great Britain (England, Scotland and Wales): cash of £10,000 or more, or its equivalent in foreign currency, must be declared to Border Force when entering or leaving the UK. Northern Ireland: cash of €10,000 or more, or its equivalent, must be declared when travelling between Northern Ireland and a non-EU country outside the UK. These thresholds apply to cash and specified monetary instruments including notes and coins, bearer bonds, and signed travellers' cheques; for Northern Ireland the obligation also covers money orders, gold coins, bullion, and prepaid cards. Declarations are administered by HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) through Border Force and derive from UK anti-money laundering legislation.
Rules for residents holding foreign currency
UK residents may hold and maintain foreign currency accounts freely with authorised banks and financial institutions.
Rules for non-residents
Non-residents may open and operate foreign currency accounts and transfer funds freely without prior approval.
Business invoicing and settlement in foreign currency
Business transactions may be invoiced and settled in foreign currency or GBP. Cross-border payments and repatriation of funds are handled freely through authorised financial institutions.
Exchange controls and approval requirements
The United Kingdom maintains no general exchange controls. The regime is fully liberalised for both current and capital account transactions.
Banking and conversion practicality
Foreign exchange conversion and transfers are widely available through banks, licensed money exchange houses, and ATMs throughout the UK.

Practical Summaries

Traveler summary

Travelers may bring in or take out unlimited amounts of foreign currency and British Pounds. No declaration is required for amounts below £10,000 (Great Britain) or €10,000 (Northern Ireland, when travelling to or from non-EU countries outside the UK).

Business summary

Businesses and individuals enjoy full freedom to hold, transfer, convert and repatriate foreign currency through authorised banks and financial institutions.
