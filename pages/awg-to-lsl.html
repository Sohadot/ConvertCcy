What an exchange rate means

An exchange rate is the price of one currency expressed in terms of another. It tells you how many units of the second currency you would receive in exchange for one unit of the first.

Exchange rates are not fixed. They move constantly as financial markets reassess value in response to data such as inflation reports, interest rate decisions, trade balances, and shifts in investor sentiment.

Reference rate vs bank rate

The reference exchange rate is useful because it shows the underlying market relationship between two currencies without adding a customer-specific fee structure.

Banks, cards, travel desks, and money-transfer providers often apply a spread or markup on top of the market relationship, so the final customer rate can be less favorable than what you see here.

ConvertCCY focuses on reference exchange rates for informational comparison. Banks, cards, and transfer services may offer different customer rates after spreads or service charges.
