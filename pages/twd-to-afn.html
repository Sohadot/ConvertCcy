What an exchange rate means

An exchange rate is the price of one currency expressed in another currency. In simple terms, it shows how much of one unit is needed to buy a unit of another.

Exchange rates can move because financial markets constantly reassess value using information such as inflation, economic growth, trade conditions, interest-rate policy, and risk sentiment.

Reference rate vs bank rate

The reference exchange rate is useful because it shows the market relationship between two currencies without adding a customer-specific fee structure.

Banks, cards, travel desks, and money-transfer providers often apply a spread or markup on top of the market relationship, so the final customer rate can be less favorable.

ConvertCCY focuses on reference exchange rates for informational comparison. Banks, cards, and transfer services may offer different customer rates after spreads or service charges.

What moves currencies

Like other currency pairs, this rate does not move randomly; it tends to respond to identifiable macro and market forces.

Currencies are influenced by interest rates, inflation, central-bank policy, trade balances, capital flows, and broader investor sentiment.

Some currencies are also sensitive to commodities, geopolitical events, or safe-haven demand during periods of global uncertainty.
